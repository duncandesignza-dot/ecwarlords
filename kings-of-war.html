What is Kings of War?

Kings of War is Mantic Games' fantasy mass-battle game. Your army fights in regiments - blocks of troops that move and fight as one - so even big battles play quickly and cleanly.

It's known for letting you use fantasy miniatures from almost any range, which makes it a popular home for older collections.

  • Publisher Mantic Games
  • Players 2
  • Game time About 2 hours
  • Style Fantasy mass battle

The setting

The world of Pannithor, shaped by war between gods.

Pannithor

A world at war

Kings of War is set in Pannithor, a fantasy world scarred by the God War - a conflict between the Shining Ones and the Wicked Ones that shattered the land and left ancient grudges behind.

The armies

Good, Neutral and Evil

Every army belongs to one of three alignments. The holy knights of Basilea and the Dwarfs stand against the demons of the Abyss and the Undead, while others - like the Ogres and Kingdoms of Men - fight for whoever suits them.

The factions

Mantic supports a huge range of armies, so there is almost certainly one that fits the models you already own.

Good

Defenders of the light

Basilea, Dwarfs, Elves, Northern Alliance, Order of the Green Lady, Salamanders, Sylvan Kin and the Trident Realm of Neritica.

Neutral

Swords for hire

Kingdoms of Men, Ogres, Free Dwarfs, the Herd, League of Rhordia and Ratkin.

Evil

Darkness rising

Forces of the Abyss, Abyssal Dwarfs, Undead, Empire of Dust, Nightstalkers, Orcs, Goblins, Twilight Kin, Varangur and Riftforged Orcs.

How a game plays

Players take full turns, moving, shooting and fighting with their whole army before handing over.

Move and charge

Order each regiment to advance, charge, turn or hold. Charges happen in the movement phase.

Shoot

Ranged units fire at enemies in range. Roll to hit, then roll to damage against the target's defence.

Melee and nerve

Units in combat attack, then the damaged unit takes a Nerve test - it may hold, waver or rout off the table.

No removing models

Damage, not casualties

Regiments don't lose models one at a time. Damage builds up on the unit until it breaks - which keeps the game moving fast.

One player rolls

Quick and clean

In combat and shooting, only the attacking player rolls dice. It keeps turns short, even with big armies on the table.

Army building

Points and unit sizes

Units come as troops, regiments, hordes and legions, each with a points cost. Players agree a size, often around 2000 points.

Missions

Scenarios

Scenarios ask you to hold ground, grab loot or break the enemy line, so manoeuvre matters as much as brute force.

The hobby side

Massed ranks look spectacular painted up on movement trays.

Build

Regiments on trays

Models are based together on regiment bases or movement trays, which makes rank-and-file units quick to move.

Paint

Paint in batches

Big blocks of troops are perfect for batch painting - a few colours across a whole regiment looks great from across the table.

Use what you have

Any fantasy range

Because units are just bases, old fantasy collections from other games often work with little or no change.
