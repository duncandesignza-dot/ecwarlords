What is Warhammer 40,000?

Warhammer 40,000 is Games Workshop's science-fiction wargame. Two or more players field armies of painted miniatures - Space Marines, Orks, Tyranids, Chaos and many more - and battle over objectives on a tabletop covered in terrain.

It's the club's flagship game: you'll find it on more tables than anything else, from first learning games to fully painted tournament armies.

  • Publisher Games Workshop
  • Players 2+
  • Game time 2-3 hours
  • Style Sci-fi battles

The setting

The galaxy of the far future, where there is only war.

The Imperium

Humanity at bay

The Imperium of Man spans a million worlds, ruled in the name of the Emperor, who has sat motionless on the Golden Throne of Terra for ten thousand years. It survives through faith, fear and endless war, defended by the superhuman Space Marines, the countless regiments of the Astra Militarum and the war machines of the Adeptus Mechanicus.

Chaos

The Ruinous Powers

Beyond reality lies the Warp, home of the four Chaos Gods - Khorne, Nurgle, Tzeentch and Slaanesh. Ten thousand years ago half the Space Marine Legions turned traitor in the Horus Heresy. Their descendants still wage the Long War against the Imperium, alongside daemons and corrupted war machines.

The Xenos

Alien empires

Humanity is far from alone. The ancient Aeldari fight to survive their fallen empire, the Necrons wake from tomb worlds after sixty million years, endless Ork hordes live for the fight, Tyranid hive fleets devour whole planets and the T'au spread their Greater Good.

Right now

A galaxy split in two

A vast warp storm, the Great Rift, has torn the galaxy in half. The resurrected Primarch Roboute Guilliman leads the Imperium's fightback, while Chaos, Necrons and Tyranids press in from every side - plenty of reasons to put armies on the table.

How a game plays

Two armies, a table full of terrain and a handful of dice. Players take turns, and each turn runs through five phases.

Command

Gain Command Points to spend on Stratagems - special tricks and abilities - and check whether battered units hold their nerve.

Movement

Move your units across the battlefield, into cover, onto objectives or into position for the next phase.

Shooting

Fire at enemy units. Roll to hit, roll to wound, then your opponent rolls saves to see what survives.

Charge

Roll the dice to see if your units make it into combat with the enemy.

Fight

Units in combat swing their swords, claws and chainaxes. The same hit, wound and save rolls decide the result.

Missions

Win on objectives

Most games last five battle rounds. You score victory points by holding objectives and completing secondary missions, so destroying the enemy isn't the only way to win - and often not the best one.

Army building

Points and lists

Every unit has a points cost. Players agree a size - smaller games around 1000 points, a standard game around 2000 - and build a list to match. Combat Patrol boxes give you a ready-made small army to start with.

Ways to play

Matched, narrative or open

Matched play is balanced and competitive, as in leagues and tournaments. Crusade is a narrative campaign where your units gain experience and scars from game to game. Open play is anything goes.

Learning curve

Easier than it looks

The core rules fit in a short booklet, and your army's datasheets hold everything else. After a learning game or two with someone guiding you, it clicks.

The hobby side

For many players the models matter as much as the games. You build, paint and own every army you field.

Build

Kits and conversions

Models come as plastic kits on frames. Clip, clean and glue them together - and once you're comfortable, kitbash parts from different kits to make characters that are entirely yours.

Paint

From tabletop to display

Undercoat, base colours, a wash and a highlight will get an army looking great on the table. Some members stop there; others chase display-level detail. Both are welcome, and there's always someone at club night happy to share tips.

Base and customise

Your colours, your story

Basing ties an army together, and many players invent their own chapter, regiment or warband with its own colour scheme and history. See what our members have painted in the Armoury.
